The Tradition & Origins

Every Stitch Tells a Tale of Devotion

Sustainable Upcycling Heritage

Originally created by Bengali women using layers of soft, worn sarees and dhotis stitched together with threads pulled from saree borders. It remains one of the world's oldest forms of zero-waste textile art.

The Poetry of 'Naksha'

The term 'Nakshi' comes from the Persian word 'Naksha', meaning artistic pattern. Artisans translate memories, monsoon rains, village life, and cosmic symbols directly onto cloth without pre-drawn stencils.

Heirlooms of Love

Historically gifted to newborns or brides as blessings, each Kantha quilt contains hundreds of thousands of microscopic 'Kantha Stitches' (running stitches), creating a unique rippled, tactile texture.

“

"Look at the Kantha quilt—it is not merely cloth; it is a mother's lullaby woven into thread, a bride's quiet longing, and a village's living history."

— Jasimuddin, Renowned Bengali Poet (Nokshi Kanthar Math)

Padma (The Sacred Lotus)

Traditional Center Motif

The lotus motif is central to almost all traditional Nakshi Kanthas. Placed right at the center of the quilt, it symbolizes the sacred cosmos, purity emerging from mud, and divine harmony. The petals are rendered with concentric running stitches called 'Chala Stitches'.

Stitching Technique

Stitched using fine red and indigo silk threads in circular progression. Outer borders are highlighted with 'Bhorat' (fill-in running stitch).
